# Naming vocabulary: what needs a user-facing name

> **The names in force are the package's (since 2026-11-19).** `packages/vocabulary/vocabulary.json` (`@armature/vocabulary`, ADR-0073) holds every name chosen here as a term, with the reserved and the avoided words; this document is the reasoning behind them. `npm run check:vocabulary` fails in CI when the two disagree: every name in a Chosen cell of §§4 to 15 is a term of the file, and every term's name is in its row. So a name changes in one pull request touching the row's Chosen cell, the file's term and a dated line in `packages/vocabulary/CHANGES.md`. A Chosen cell may hold several names, separated by commas or semicolons, and a few connecting words the check knows ("until requests are built", "(people)"); the header of `scripts/check_vocabulary.js` has the rule.
>
> **Carried to Armature's own pages on 2026-11-19:** the desktop app's pages, components and tray, the desktop core's messages, the viewer's pages and the guide (`docs/desktop-program.md`) use the chosen names, and `check:vocabulary` fails on an avoided word in any of the three surfaces it reads. The names that wait on ADR-0072 appear by their names for until then ("Merge into main", "Preview merge"). In the app they reach people with the first version after 0.1.4. The refusals of the hub's own workspace routes (`app/lib/api/workspace/`, `routes/workspace.ts`), which a page shows as given, followed the same day; they say "the instance", since a hub's message cannot know the instance's own name. The shell's diagnostic lines followed in 0.1.5 (PR #184), and the documents and plans later that day: "pairing key" everywhere but the identifiers, the accepted ADRs and the dated records; "update from `main`", "merge into `main`" and "the desktop app" in the README, the explainer, `deployment.md` and `demo-coverage.md`, while `api.md`, the schema guide and the plans keep ADR-0065's terms beside the routes they name; "operator" for whoever runs the server, with the role keeping "administrator" (collision 6). Not yet carried: those routes' messages about the hub itself, which tool authors read; and Quiver, which carries its own. **ADR-0072 was built on 2026-11-20**, and the names that waited on it are in use: the twelve terms lost their `until`, "Merge into main" and "Preview merge" gave way to "Open merge request into <branch>" and the merge request's health, "Clone a branch" went and "clone" is avoided for people, and the pages say "Link to this computer", "Unlink from this computer", "Unlink from the other computer", "Your unlinked workspaces", "Linked to another computer" and "Not linked to this computer" (`packages/vocabulary/CHANGES.md`, 2026-11-20). Rows whose Notes say "not built" record the day the names were chosen. The "Names in use now" column below is therefore the record of what the names were on 2026-11-18, not of what the pages say today.
>
> **Status (2026-11-18): decided.** Amos chose every name on 2026-11-18, through a decision page: all 23 collisions in §1 as recommended, and every other row as recommended, none reopened. The **Chosen** column records each row's names. Carrying them to the program's pages, the guide, the documents and the tools is the work that followed (the note above says how far it has gone), and the names that depend on ADR-0072 take effect when it is built, with each row's name for until then. Started when Quiver resumed on the desktop program and showed one thing going by several names across the program's pages, the guide, the plans and Quiver. The **Recommended** column is this document's proposal, argued in §2 and backed by the evidence in §3. Decisions of 2026-11-18 already bear on the names, recorded and not yet built. A workspace's merges are with its *parent* branch (ADR-0065 decision 6, later change of that day). A workspace is linked to one computer, moved by unlinking it and linking it again, and its work leaves through a merge request (ADR-0072, accepted 2026-11-19, not built). So the workspace verbs are named for the parent, which today is always `main` (§2, "Branches and workspaces"). Where a row's name depends on ADR-0072, the row gives the name to use until it is built and the name once it is. A decision then updates every place the term appears (CLAUDE.md, "Documents and records"). The "Names in use now" column lists the places found on 2026-11-18, checked against the code and the documents that day; a search for the term before the change finds any added since. The recommendations were revised the same day after a review of every citation, with the eleven changes Amos accepted from it. ADR-0073 (accepted 2026-11-18, built 2026-11-19) moved the names into the toolkit as `@armature/vocabulary`, with its reserved and avoided words: this document keeps the reasoning, and a check in CI holds the two together. A later change to a name is a new decision: its row's Chosen cell changes, with the reason in the pull request.

## What this list covers

Every thing, state or action that a person meets by name in at least one of these places:

- the desktop program's pages (`desktop/src/pages/`, `desktop/src/components/`);
- the viewer's pages (`app/app/`);
- a tool beside the program (Quiver, CoQui, the Model Context Protocol (MCP) server);
- the person-facing guides (`docs/desktop-program.md`, `docs/how-armature-works.md`, `docs/deployment.md` for administrators);
- refusal codes, which the program shows beside every refusal (`docs/desktop-program.md` § "When something is refused").

The list was built from `docs/deployment.md` § "The terms", `docs/workspace-hub-plan.md` §§2 to 9, `docs/desktop-program.md`, the strings on the shell's pages, the viewer's headings and titles, the schema's classes and enumerations, ADR-0068 (authorization) and ADR-0070 (checks), and Quiver's `docs/plan.md` §3 and `docs/program-notes.md`. Terms that never reach a person are listed last (§17), so nobody names them by accident.

"Pages" below means the desktop program's pages unless the row says the viewer. Bracketed references such as [S2] point to the sources in §3.

## 1. The collisions found

These are the problems the list turned up. Each one points to its row below, and the arrow gives the resolution, recommended and chosen by Amos on 2026-11-18.

1. **Instance, remote or remote hub.** The guide says "your instance" throughout, and so does the Connect page ("Connect to your instance"). Every other page says "the remote": "Clone a branch the remote has", "Who are you on this remote?", "The remote moved", "N records changed on the remote since your last push", "Everything here is on the remote", and the core's own refusal messages, which the app shows word for word ("The remote at … could not be reached"). The plans say "remote hub". A person meets two words for the shared server within one sitting. (§4, *The shared installation*) → **the instance's own name in sentences, "instance" as the general word; "remote" leaves every person-facing surface.**
2. **Workspace hub, desktop hub or "the hub".** The ADRs and plans say "workspace hub". Quiver's plan §3 heading says "desktop hub", which is also what Amos calls it. The program's pages say just "the hub" ("The hub is running", "Start the hub"). In the documents, "a hub" alone means *any* hub, remote or local. (§4, *The hub on your computer*) → **"desktop hub", matching "Armature Desktop"; tools mostly name the app instead.**
3. **"The remote moved" means the wrong thing to readers.** The badge on the Workspaces page means the workspace's *own branch* on the instance changed since this machine last pushed, cloned or brought `main` in (another machine, or another grant holder, wrote to it; `routes/workspace.ts`, the status route). The guide reads it as "whether `main` has moved on the instance" (`docs/desktop-program.md` § "Your workspaces"), which the status does not report (Quiver's `program-notes.md` §1). The label let the guide's author misread it. (§8, *The workspace's branch moved*) → **"Changed elsewhere", and the guide corrected. Once workspaces are linked (ADR-0072), only the linked app can move the branch, and the state gives way to "Linked to another computer".**
4. **Draft.** Three meanings: the `Draft` status of an item and of an objective (`ItemStatus`, `ObjectiveState`); Quiver's *draft*, an edit not yet valid enough to commit and kept only on Quiver's side; and an AI drafter's proposal ("a kept draft", Quiver `program-notes.md` §6). Quiver can hold a draft of a `Draft` item. (§7, *Commit*; §12, *Item status*; §16) → **`Draft` stays the status, as item banks use it [S25][S26]; Quiver's becomes an "uncommitted edit"; the drafter's output is a "proposal".**
5. **Save, commit or write.** The workspace hub turns every write into a commit. Quiver calls committing "saving to the workspace" ("not yet saved to the workspace"). The program uses "Save" and "Saved." for its settings and personal checks, which are files and not commits. The guide says "your tools commit as you work". (§6, *A commit*; §7, *Commit*) → **"commit" for the record everywhere; "save" only for things that are not commits.**
6. **Administrator.** It means the person who runs an instance ("ask your administrator", the guide; `deployment.md` is "for the administrator"). It is also the `Administrator` grant role, which only some such people hold, and which people who never touch the server can hold. Next to it are "operator" (who runs the operator tools on the server) and "maintainer". (§9) → **"administrator" is the role holder, who manages people and roles; the person who runs the server is the "operator".**
7. **Profile.** It means the export's profile (`full`, `pseudonymous`, `institutional`; ADR-0031). It also means the connection profile, what the program shows before connecting (`profile_changed`). And a person's own profile (`profile=full` on the users list). (§10, *What the instance shows before connecting*; §12, *Export profile*) → **"instance details" and "privacy level"; "profile" stays in identifiers only.**
8. **Check.** A check is a tool that reports a verdict (ADR-0070). The same word is also used for "Check again" (sign-in), "Check for updates", "Checking…", the operator's `check` command, and the lettered platform checks. (§11, *Other uses of "check"*) → **"check" kept for ADR-0070's checks only; the other buttons reworded.**
9. **Verdict.** It is a check's outcome (the `CheckVerdicts` component; "reports a verdict" in the guide; `conclusion`, with values `pass`, `fail`, `neutral` and `error`, in the API). It is also an attestation's `verdict` (`Affirmed`, `Declined`). And the viewer uses it for a coverage level ("An objective declared by two modules wears the weaker verdict", the coverage lens's note) and in the Walkthrough. (§11, *A check's outcome*; §12, *Attestation*) → **a check has a "result"; an attestation, shown as a "sign-off", has the verdict; a coverage level is a "level".**
10. **Going past a check.** "Deliver anyway" and "Push anyway" are the buttons; "set aside" is the word of the guide and of the pages (the badge "<result>, set aside", "Set aside: <reason>"); `overrides` is the API's; "Why <check> is set aside" is the field. (§11, *Going past a check*) → **"override", with "Merge anyway" and "Push anyway"; the badges say "overridden".**
11. **Bring in `main`, pull, sync or merge.** The program says "Bring in main" and "Brought main in". Quiver's earlier model said "pull" and "sync" and planned a "Sync button". The API says merge. The phrase is hard to use as a noun ("a bring-in") or in the past tense. (§7, *Bring in main*) → **"Update from <parent>", which reads "Update from main" today: Figma's verified label [S2], and Abstract's pair "Update from Main" and "Update from Parent" [S5].**
12. **Deliver or merge.** The button says "Deliver into main". The reason note on the same page says it "is recorded on the merge into main". The guide's opening says you deliver "for a maintainer to see", which suggests a request for review. Its own § "Your workspaces" says only a maintainer can deliver, which describes a merge. And to an instructional designer, "delivery" means putting a course in front of learners [S23], which is the sense of the viewer's coverage figure "Delivered" (approved items in approved placements, beside "Projected"). (§7, *Deliver*) → **a merge request: "Open merge request into <parent>", which whoever may merge into the parent merges: its owner or a maintainer of it, and for `main` a maintainer only (ADR-0068 decision 4; ADR-0072, accepted 2026-11-19, not built). Until requests are built, the button a maintainer of `main` uses reads "Merge into main".**
13. **Remove, discard or delete.** "Remove" a workspace, which deletes its branch on the instance or keeps it. "Discard and remove" a workspace with unpushed commits. "Remove" a row of personal checks. Quiver "discards" a draft. The API "deletes" a branch. (§7) → **"remove" acts on this computer, "delete" on the instance, and "discard" gives up work that never left this computer.**
14. **Identifier.** "Your identifier on the remote" (Settings, under the sign-in-free mode), "Your identifier for tools" (Pairing), the `Armature-User` header, `externalId`, an email address, the provider's `sub`, and the *handle* that starts a branch name (`jdoe_` in `jdoe_unit-3`). The handle is the part of the email before the @. (§9, *Identifier*) → **"your ID on <instance>", one name for both fields.**
15. **Log.** The program's **Log** page shows the program's diagnostic lines. A branch's log (`GET /branches/:name/log`) is its list of commits. Quiver reads the second as "the branch's log". (§6, *A branch's log*; §7, *Read the app's log*) → **"history" for commits; "Diagnostics" for the page.**
16. **Note.** A `DesignNote` is a design record. Quiver's commit *note* is the optional sentence added to a generated message. A check's *summary* sits close to both. (§6, *Commit message*; §12, *Design note*) → **"design note" for the record; Quiver's sentence is part of the "message".**
17. **Record, document or artifact.** The pages say "records" ("Brought main in: 12 records changed."). The viewer says "Artifacts" and "Relationships" (the Design Graph's filter headings), "Record details" and "The document on the hub". The API and the schema say document, design record and artifact. (§12, *Design record*) → **"record" for people; "document" for the API only.**
18. **Version or revision.** The program has a version (0.1.4). A hub has a revision ("Its hub's revision", "The remote serves"; the client revision 2026-11-16). The refusal `unsupported_version` is about revisions. (§14) → **people see "version" for the app and "compatible" for the pair; "API revision" only in the details.**
19. **Rules.** The guide and Quiver say "rules". The program says "Broken rules". The code and CLAUDE.md say "invariants" and numbered "constraints". The API says `invariant_violation`. The lint plan says "lint". (§13) → **"rules" for people.**
20. **Tool, client or plugin.** The guide and pages say "tool" ("Every paired tool"). The documents for developers say "client" and "plugin", and "reference client". (§4, *Tools*) → **"tool" for people, "client" for developers, "plugin" retired.**
21. **The program's own name.** It is "Armature" (the app and window name), "Armature desktop" (the release title), "the desktop program" and "the program". Inside the code it is the shell, the core and the sidecar, which a person should never see. (§4, *The desktop program*) → **"Armature Desktop", "the desktop app".**
22. **This machine or this computer.** The pages say "This machine". Quiver's status strip says "this computer". The guide says "your machine" and "your own machine". The viewer's page is "Work from your own machine". (§4, *This computer*) → **"this computer".**
23. **Branch or workspace.** A workspace is a branch held on a computer, so in the ordinary case the two words name one line of work. A person meets both only where the design breaks: a second writer on a branch gets a refused push, with discarding as the only way out (§8, *Stranded commits*). Raised 2026-11-18. (§5, *Branch*, *Workspace*, *Link*) → **two names by place: a branch is on the instance, written directly by anyone with the right from any client; a workspace is on one computer, written only by the desktop app it is linked to (ADR-0072, accepted 2026-11-19, not built). "Branch" stays the general word.**

## 2. The recommendation as a whole

### Who reads these names

| Audience | Where they meet the names | What they already know | What follows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Designers and reviewers**: instructional designers, item writers, subject experts | Armature Desktop, Quiver, CoQui, the viewer, the desktop guide | Authoring tools (Storyline and Rise, where **Publish** means producing a course package [S21][S22]), learning management systems (where Canvas's **Sync** pushes one way and overwrites [S24]), item banks (**Draft**, **Review**, **Approved**, **Retired** [S25][S26]), Google Docs version history [S20], Slack and Notion, where a **workspace** is a team's space [S30][S31]. Some know Figma, which has the closest workflow: **Create branch**, **Update from main**, **Merge**, **Request review** [S1]–[S4] | Plain words, and Figma's dialect of version control where there is one. Avoid every word their other tools already use for something else |
| **Learning engineers and researchers** | The viewer, history, exports, `docs/how-armature-works.md` | Version control as a research object. Armature's explainer uses "commit" or "commits" 236 times and "record" or "records" 196 times | Keep "commit" and "as of a commit" visible: they are what a design history is made of (ADR-0025) |
| **Administrators and operators** | `docs/deployment.md`, the operator tools, sign-in setup | Self-hosted servers: GitLab's **instance** and **Admin area** [S18], Moodle's **Site administration** [S32], OpenID Connect | "Instance", "hub", "operator", "sign-in provider" |
| **Tool authors** | `docs/api.md`, `docs/schema-guide.md`, refusal codes | Git and HTTP | Identifiers stay as built; the reference maps each person-facing word to its route and code |

### Principles

1. **One name per concept, used by every surface a person reads.** The app, the tools, the viewer and the guides say the same word. Developer documents use it in their prose too, and add the identifier beside it.
2. **Identifiers do not change.** Route paths (`/workspace/:name/deliver`), refusal codes (`bring_in_first`), fields (`asOf`) and enumeration values (`InReview`) stay. Renaming them would be a client revision for no gain to a person. The app already shows a refusal's words first and its code second; the words follow this list. Where an identifier and the person-facing word differ, `docs/api.md` says so once.
3. **Stay off words the audience already uses for something else.** For designers, "publish" and "delivery" mean producing and running a course [S21][S23], and "sync" means Canvas's overwrite [S24]. For the item-bank statuses, "draft", "approved" and "retired" are taken [S25][S26]. "Verdict" and "profile" already mean two things each here.
4. **Name places by where they are**: "this computer", the instance by its own name, and `main`. "Local" and "remote" stay in the code.
5. **Each verb keeps one scope.** **Save** is for settings and files that are not commits. **Remove** acts on this computer, and **delete** on the instance. **Discard** gives up work that never left this computer, which covers Quiver's uncommitted edit and a workspace's unpushed commits alike.
6. **The workspace verbs form a set**: **Push**, **Update from <parent>**, **Open merge request into <parent>**, and, for whoever may merge into the parent, **Merge**. Each names its direction and the branch at the other end, as Figma's labels do [S2][S3]. While every workspace's parent is `main`, they read "Update from main" and "Open merge request into main". Moving a workspace adds **Unlink from this computer** and **Link to this computer**, and **Unlink from the other computer** for one out of reach.
7. **Full words and the system's dates.** Messages and labels use no contractions ("cannot", "could not", "is not"), as the pages do today. Dates follow the system's own format, as the pages already do; the examples here are written the American way ("Nov 18").

### The choice of metaphor

The cycle is a version-control cycle whatever it is called. The choice is which dialect to speak.

| Concept | **Set A: branch and merge, said plainly** (recommended) | **Set B: save and send** | Rejected sets |
|---|---|---|---|
| The record of a change | commit | saved change, "save" | **Publish and draft** (content-management style): "publish" means producing a course [S21][S22] or making content live [S27][S28]; "draft" is an item status [S25] |
| Sending it to the instance | Push [S8][S12] | Send to <instance> | **Check out and check in** (SharePoint, Perforce [S14], Gluon's "Checkin"): suggests locking, and "check" is taken by checks |
| Taking in the parent's new work | Update from <parent> [S2][S5][S10] | Get updates from <parent> | **Sync** (Canvas [S24], VS Code's two-way "Sync Changes" [S12]): either it overwrites or it hides which way work moves |
| Combining into the parent | Merge into <parent> [S3][S6][S8] | Add to <parent> | **Deliver**: in no comparable product (§3, "What the research did not find"), and "delivery" means teaching the course [S23] |
| Asking for that | Merge request [S42] | Ask to add | |
| Trying it first | The request's health, current on every look | Preview | |
| The shared installation | <instance name>; "instance" | <instance name>; "server" [S29] | |

**Why Set A.** It is the dialect that design tools with real branching already teach designers (Figma verified [S1]–[S4]; Abstract from its indexed help, its site being offline [S5][S6]; Onshape [S16]). It keeps "commit", which the viewer, the history, `asOf` and the research path all depend on. And it agrees with the API's own words, so a tool author and a designer describe the same step the same way. Set B is easier on first contact, but it costs three things. "Save" would have to mean a commit in the app and a file write in its settings. "Send" sounds like messaging a person. "Add to main" hides that the step can conflict and be refused. A tool may still take Set B's tone in its own help text, as long as its buttons use Set A's words.

### Branches and workspaces

Decided by Amos on 2026-11-18. The parent is ADR-0065 decision 6's later change of that day; the link, the move and merge requests are ADR-0072 (accepted 2026-11-19, not built). None of it is built.

- **The two are told apart by place.** A **branch** is on the instance. Anyone with the right writes it directly, from any client, while online, and it takes merges; collaboration happens there. A **workspace** is on one computer, linked to one installation of Armature Desktop, and usable offline. Its copy on the instance exists so the app can always push, and only that app writes it. A workspace is **personal**, not private: only its owner writes it, but anyone signed in can read it, as with every branch.
- **Every workspace has a parent**: the branch it started from, never another workspace. It takes updates from its parent, and its work leaves through a **merge request** into its parent. Today every parent is `main`, so the labels read "Update from main" and "Open merge request into main". They are named for the parent so that nothing is renamed when the build lands.
- **Why only the linked app writes a workspace.** A push replays commits onto a branch that must not have moved. Any other writer moves it, and the app's unpushed commits are stranded, with discarding as the only way out (§8, *Stranded commits*). That holds for another client, a tool holding the owner's credential, or the owner on a second computer. Online writers don't have this problem, so branches stay open to direct writes.
- **Moving a workspace is two steps.** **Unlink from this computer**, once everything is pushed; then, on the other computer, **Link to this computer** from the list of your unlinked workspaces. If the first computer is lost, **Unlink from the other computer** releases it, and the confirmation says that computer's unpushed commits can no longer be pushed.
- **Merge requests.** Someone who may not merge into a branch, `main` included, opens a **merge request**. Whether it would merge cleanly (conflicts, broken rules, required checks) is worked out each time anyone looks. Whoever may merge into the target **merges** it or **closes** it: the target's **owner** or a **maintainer** of it, and for `main` a maintainer only (ADR-0068 decision 4). The person who opened it, or the source's owner, may also close it. Conflicts are resolved by the workspace's owner, by updating from the parent.

### The whole product in one pass

Read this as a test: every name below is the recommended one, and none means two things.

> Dana installs **Armature Desktop** and connects to **Westfield Armature**, reading the **instance details** first. They sign in through Westfield's **sign-in provider** and create a **workspace** from `main`, named `unit-3`; the instance calls the branch `dana_unit-3`. Quiver needs the **pairing key** and Dana's **ID on Westfield Armature**, both on the **Pairing** page. In Quiver, an item that still breaks a **rule** is an **uncommitted edit**; when it is valid and Dana moves on, Quiver **commits** it. Armature Desktop shows "3 to push", and Dana **pushes**. Later the workspace shows "**main has updates**", so Dana **updates from main**. One **conflict** comes up: in Quiver, beside the **starting point**, Dana chooses **Keep yours** for the stem and **Take main's** for option c. Dana **opens a merge request into main**, and its health shows a **required check** with no **result** yet. When it **passes**, Dana, a **maintainer** of `main`, **merges** the request; Dana's colleague, who is not one, would open the same request for a maintainer to merge. To carry on from a laptop, Dana **unlinks** the workspace **from this computer**, and on the laptop **links** it **to this computer** from the list of unlinked workspaces. When the unit is finished, Dana **removes** the workspace **from this computer**, and its branch is **deleted** on Westfield Armature because `main` holds all of it. A researcher later opens the item's **history** in the viewer, **as of** the commit a dataset names, and sees "proposed by Quiver drafter, for Dana" and a reviewer's **sign-off**, affirmed.

> For an A/B test of two assessment methods for module 3, Amos creates two **branches**, `bank3-a` and `bank3-b`. Amos and Lee each create a **workspace** with `bank3-a` as its **parent**. Each works in their own workspace, pushes, and opens a **merge request** into `bank3-a`, which Amos, its owner, merges; nobody pushes to `bank3-a` itself. A testing tool reads both branches. When method B is chosen, Amos opens a merge request from `bank3-b` into `main` and merges it. `bank3-a` stays on the instance as the record of the method not taken.

The first paragraph assumes one piece of hub work, "main has updates", which the status does not report today (§8). The rest assumes the decisions of 2026-11-18 above, none of them built yet.

## 17. Terms that need no user-facing name

These stay in the code and the developer documents. If one starts showing up on a page or in a refusal message, it needs a row above first.

- **Store**, **host**, **server** (in the `deployment.md` sense), **schema**: administrators, operators and developers only.
- **The shell**, **the core**, **the sidecar**, **the relay** (`relay.rs`): the desktop app's internals (ADR-0069).
- **Capability**, **revision negotiation**, **`GET /discover`**: tool authors only (ADR-0059).
- **Trailer** (`Armature-Workspace-Commit`, `Armature-Authored-At`, `Armature-Check`, and others): carried in commit messages. A history view shows their meaning ("made", "for", "via"), not the trailers themselves.
- **`ETag`**, **`If-Match`**, **412**: the mechanism behind "Changed elsewhere".
- **The write queue**, **the transient branch**, **`at.<commit>` branches**, **the commit table** (`push.json`), **the in-flight marker**, **the split rule** and its parts: internals of the hub and of push.
- **Resolver** (`header`, `oidc`): operators see the setting. People see "No sign-in (for testing only)" or a sign-in provider.
- **The mirror of `main`** on a desktop hub: an internal copy of the schema and the registry.
- **Ownerless branches**, **migration window**: administrators only (ADR-0068).
- **The snapshot budget** (`ARMATURE_SNAPSHOTS`): surfaced only as "Older commits kept offline".
- **Refusal codes not in the guide's table** (`remote_only`, `workspace_only`, `program_action`, `type_conflict`, …): tool authors meet them in `docs/api.md`. If the app starts showing one to a person, it needs words in the guide's table first.
